Joining over the internet now just works, with no relay and no port forwarding.

**[Download GodotCoop-windows.zip](https://github.com/i-Roboticist/godot-coop/releases/download/v1.1.2/GodotCoop-windows.zip)**, unzip it and run `GodotCoop.exe`. **Both the host and the people joining need 1.1.2** (update the plugin in your project from the app). It still works with 1.1.x for everything else, but older hosts make invites that only work on their own network.

- **Internet joining without setup.** Before, an invite only worked across networks if the host's router allowed UPnP or someone ran a relay. Now the two apps meet through free public message services (three MQTT brokers and ntfy.sh, used together so one being down doesn't matter), learn their public addresses from STUN servers, and punch a direct connection through both routers. It's the same technique Tailscale uses, without accounts. Everything exchanged on those services is encrypted with the invite's secret, and your project goes straight between your PCs as before.
- The joining side's router can open a port with UPnP too, which gets through even when the host's router is strict.
- IPv6 is used when both sides have it.
- The host's dock shows a new **Internet** line: your public address, or a warning if your network blocks this kind of connection.
- When a join can't get through, the message says why (host not answering, old version, strict routers, blocked network) and what to try.
- Tested joining in both directions between a home network and a cloud data center, with UPnP off. A new manual workflow (`internet-test.yml`) and `app/tests/inet_probe.gd` let anyone repeat that test.
